The tree is down, the lights are off, and every ornament, garland, and string of fairy lights is sitting in a pile on your living room floor. In a small apartment that pile has nowhere to hide — and if you shove it all into one random box, you will pay for it next December with broken ornaments and a tangled mess. These christmas decoration storage ideas fit the storage you actually have: one closet shelf, the space under your bed, or the top of a kitchen cabinet. This is holiday decoration storage small spaces can actually use — no garage, no spare room, no storage unit required. Work through the 16 ideas and everything will be packed, labeled, and out of sight in a single afternoon.

![Labeled bins of Christmas ornaments, wreaths, and lights on a closet shelf](/images/christmas-decoration-storage-small-apartments-hero.jpg)

## The Post-Holiday Sort: What Stays, What Goes Before You Pack

Packing starts with sorting, not with buying boxes.

### Idea 1: Sort into keep, donate, or toss piles

Spread everything on the floor and make three piles: keep what you put up this year and love; donate duplicates, unhung gifts, and anything unused for two Christmases; toss anything broken, stained, or missing pieces. A light string with half its bulbs dead is not worth a storage slot.

### Idea 2: Photograph each display before you take it down

Photograph the tree, the mantel, and the table before you dismantle them, and tuck a note with the photo's location on your phone into the matching bin. Next December you will not have to remember how the garland draped or which ornaments went where — you just follow your own picture. Two minutes now saves a frustrating hour later.

## Ornament Storage: Divided Boxes vs DIY Egg-Carton Trick

Ornaments are the most fragile thing you own at Christmas, so the container matters most here.

### Idea 3: Use a divided ornament box for glass ornaments

A divided ornament box, with cardboard or fabric compartments sized for 3- to 4-inch ornaments, is the safest home for anything breakable. Each ornament sits in its own cell, so nothing knocks against anything else. Pick a rigid-lid box with a handle, and store it flat, never on its side. One standard box holds about 24 to 30 ornaments and slides neatly onto a closet shelf.

![Divided ornament box with glass baubles in individual compartments](/images/christmas-decoration-storage-small-apartments-01.jpg)

### Idea 4: Pack sturdy ornaments in egg cartons

You do not need a special box for shatterproof ornaments. Clean egg cartons hold small baubles perfectly, one per cup, and a stack of them fits inside any shoebox. For larger plastic ornaments, line a small bin with paper cupcake liners — each liner cradles one ornament and stops it rolling. Wrap any ornament with a delicate finish in tissue paper first.

<div class="tip"><strong>Tip:</strong> Keep ornament hooks in a small zip-top bag taped inside the ornament box lid, so you never hunt for hooks next December.</div>

## Wreath Storage That Keeps the Shape

A wreath stored badly comes out flat and shedding needles, so wreaths need a plan of their own.

### Idea 5: Hang wreaths in zippered wreath bags

A padded, zippered wreath bag protects the shape and keeps dust off. Hang it from a sturdy hook inside a closet door — hanging beats stacking, because nothing presses down on the foliage. Measure your wreath first: most apartment wreaths are 24 to 30 inches across, and bags are sold by diameter.

### Idea 6: Use a wreath box for heavy decorated wreaths

If your wreath is loaded with pinecones, berries, or large bows, a rigid round box is safer than a soft bag. The hard shell stops the decorations from being crushed by anything placed on top. Slide the box flat on a top closet shelf, where nothing else needs to sit above it.

## Light Reels and Cord Wraps: No More Tangled Strings

Tangles happen because loose strings are coiled and tossed in a box — a reel or a wrap fixes that.

### Idea 7: Wind lights onto a reel or paper tube

A plastic light reel — or a sturdy cardboard tube from a roll of wrapping paper — turns a string of lights into a neat spool. Wind slowly and tape the plug end to the tube so it does not unwind. Label each reel with its length and spot. One reel per string, no exceptions — two strings on one reel is how tangles start.

### Idea 8: Coil cords in figure-eights with velcro ties

For extension cords, garland wire, and tree-light cords, use the figure-eight coil: loop the cord in a figure eight around your hand and elbow, which prevents the twisting a round coil causes. Secure each coil with two reusable velcro ties and store the coils upright in a small bin, plugs facing up. You can pull one cord out without disturbing the rest.

## Tree Storage in a Small Apartment: Bag vs Box vs Disassembled

Your tree is the biggest item in your collection, and the right storage choice depends on its size.

### Idea 9: Use a rolling tree bag for trees up to 7 feet

A rolling tree bag with wheels is the best option for most apartment trees. It compresses the tree, protects the branches, and lets you roll it under a bed or into the back of a closet without lifting it. Look for a wide opening and sturdy handles, and cinch the compression straps before you zip it closed.

### Idea 10: Disassemble the tree into its original box

If your tree came in sections, take it apart completely — trunk sections, branch tiers, and the stand stored separately. Wrap each branch tier in an old sheet to stop needles shedding everywhere. The original box is the most space-efficient shape for a disassembled tree and slides flat under a bed or behind a wardrobe. If you threw the box away, a long under-bed bin works just as well. ## Labeled Bins by Zone: Tree, Mantel, Table, Outdoor

One giant "Christmas" box is a trap: you dig through everything to find one garland. Zone bins fix that.

### Idea 11: Pack one labeled bin per zone

Use four small bins instead of one large one: tree, mantel, table, and outdoor. Each bin holds only the decorations for that zone, plus the small extras that belong there — the tree skirt in the tree bin, the stocking holders in the mantel bin. Label each bin on two sides so you can read it from any angle.

### Idea 12: Store gift wrap in the same zone system

Gift wrap, ribbon, tape, and gift bags belong in your Christmas storage too — they are often the items scattered around the apartment all year. Stand wrapping paper rolls upright in a tall, narrow bin, with ribbon and tape in a small lidded box on top. These [15 Gift Wrap Storage Ideas for Small Spaces](/blog/gift-wrap-storage-ideas-small-spaces/) cover vertical roll storage and door-hung organizers for the smallest apartments.

<div class="tip"><strong>Tip:</strong> Write the bin's contents on masking tape stuck to the lid — peel it off and rewrite it next year if the contents change.</div>

## Where to Stash It All: Top of Closet, Under Bed, Above Cabinets

Small apartment christmas storage means using the spaces you already have but never use.

### Idea 13: Put the lightest bins on the top closet shelf

The top shelf is the hardest to reach, which makes it perfect for things you touch once a year: put the lightest bins up there — wreaths, garlands, and the ornament box. Never put the heaviest bin on the top shelf; if it falls, it can injure you and smash everything inside.

### Idea 14: Slide flat bins under the bed and over cabinets

Under-bed bins are the best home for flat, wide items: the disassembled tree, the wreath box, and the light reels laid side by side. Choose low, lidded bins about 6 inches tall, so dust stays out. Above the kitchen cabinets, use the gap below the ceiling for one or two light bins — but only if the spot stays dry and is not above the stove, where heat and grease can damage decorations. ## The January Reset: Inventory List So Next December Is Easy

The last step takes ten minutes and is the one most people skip.

### Idea 15: Tape a one-page inventory list to the biggest bin

List every bin, its zone, and its main contents on a single sheet of paper. Note anything that needs replacing — "buy 2 strings of warm white lights" or "one stocking holder cracked." Tape the list inside the lid of the largest bin. When December comes, you will know exactly what you have and what to buy.

### Idea 16: Set a reminder for the first weekend of December

Before you put the last bin away, set a phone reminder for the first Saturday in December: "Get Christmas bins down." That reminder is the difference between a relaxed decorating weekend and a last-minute scramble. Note the storage spots on the reminder so future-you does not have to remember where anything went.

## Mistakes to Avoid

**Storing decorations in a damp basement or a hot attic.** Moisture warps cardboard and grows mold on fabric ornaments; heat melts candles and warps plastic. Keep everything inside your apartment, in a dry spot.

**Mixing broken items in with good ones.** A burned-out light string packed away "to fix later" will still be broken next year. Fix it now or let it go.

**Using newspaper to wrap ornaments.** Newsprint rubs ink onto light-colored ornaments and leaves them gray. Use plain tissue paper or white packing paper instead.

**Overfilling bins until the lid bulges.** A bin that will not close properly lets in dust and crushes whatever is on top. If the lid does not sit flat, you need a second bin, not more force.

**Forgetting to label anything.** An unlabeled bin is a mystery box. Label every bin on at least two sides — you will not remember, eleven months from now.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I store Christmas decorations in a small apartment without a storage unit?

Yes. Most collections fit in three to four small bins plus a wreath bag and a tree bag, using the top of a closet, under-bed space, and the gap above kitchen cabinets. The key is sorting first and packing by zone.

### How do I keep glass ornaments from breaking in storage?

Use a divided ornament box with individual compartments, and store the box flat, never on its side. Wrap anything that does not fit a compartment in tissue paper, and keep the box where nothing heavy sits on top.

### What is the best way to store Christmas lights in a small space?

Wind each string onto its own reel or a sturdy cardboard tube, tape the plug end down, and label the reel with the length and location. Store the reels upright in a small bin. One string per reel is the rule that prevents tangles.
