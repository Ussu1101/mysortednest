Every January, the same problem shows up in small apartments: the tree comes down, the ornaments come off, and suddenly you own a pile of boxes with nowhere to go. Holiday decoration storage ideas for small apartments have to solve two problems at once — protecting fragile decorations for eleven months and finding them a home that does not eat your everyday space. This guide does both, with a quick purge, a labeling system that survives the year, packing methods that cost almost nothing, and hiding spots you probably walk past every day, so next December starts with decorating instead of untangling.

![Labeled holiday storage bins stacked neatly on a closet shelf in a small apartment](/images/holiday-decoration-storage-ideas-small-apartments-hero.jpg)

## The post-holiday purge: what actually earns a bin

Before packing, decide what deserves storage space at all — every bin you keep is one you live with all year, so the purge is the most valuable step here.

### 1. Keep only what you displayed last season

If a decoration stayed in a box through the entire holiday season, it did not earn its space. Set it aside for donation the same day you take everything down.

### 2. Retire broken, faded, and duplicate decor first

Test every light string before packing it, and throw out any strand with dead sections, frayed wire, or a plug that feels loose. Toss ornaments with cracked hooks or faded paint that bothers you every year, and anything you kept only because someone gave it to you.

## One bin per holiday: the labeling system that works

Sorting by holiday instead of by decoration type makes unpacking fast: one bin in December, one in October, instead of opening four bins to find the Halloween garland.

### 3. Assign one labeled bin to each holiday

Give Christmas, Halloween, and any other holiday you decorate for its own bin, sized to what you actually own — a 12-gallon bin is plenty for most apartment-sized Christmas collections, while a smaller 6-gallon bin usually covers Halloween. If one holiday needs two bins, label them "Christmas 1 of 2" and "Christmas 2 of 2" so you never wonder whether a bin is missing. The same label-first logic works for other seasonal items — see our [winter gear storage ideas](/blog/winter-gear-storage-ideas-small-apartments/).

### 4. Label three sides, not just the top

Bins get stacked, and the top label disappears the moment another bin sits on it. Write the holiday name, year packed, and a short contents line — "Christmas: ornaments, tree skirt, stockings" — on the front and both short ends. Mark one bin "unpack first" — the one holding the tree skirt and first ornaments — so setup starts without opening everything.

<div class="tip"><strong>Quick win:</strong> Photograph each label with your phone before stacking the bins. When a bin ends up behind furniture, the photo tells you exactly which one to dig out.</div>

## Ornament protection without buying specialty boxes

Specialty ornament boxes are bulky and pricey for an apartment. Ordinary household materials protect ornaments just as well if you pack in layers.

### 5. Wrap ornaments in tissue, then nest them in egg-carton style layers

Wrap each fragile ornament in one sheet of tissue paper, then stand them upright in a small box lined with a dish towel, separated by crumpled tissue so nothing touches its neighbor. For shatterproof ornaments, a clean egg carton or cardboard drink carrier works as a free divider — one ornament per cup, lid closed, carton inside the bin. Heaviest ornaments go at the bottom, lightest on top.

### 6. Use plastic cups and socks for the awkward pieces

Oddly shaped ornaments — finials, icicles, figurines with sticking-out parts — break because they rattle around loose. Slip each one into a clean plastic cup stuffed lightly with tissue, and stack the cups upright in a bin. Clean socks work as sleeves too: one ornament per sock, socks flat in a zip-top bag.

## Wreaths, garlands, and awkward shapes solved

Wreaths and garlands come out of storage crushed because they get shoved on top of a bin as an afterthought. They need a hanging spot or a flat, protected space.

### 7. Hang wreaths on a hook inside a garment bag

Hang each wreath on a sturdy adhesive hook or an over-the-door hook on the inside of a closet door, then slip a garment bag or a large trash bag over it, open at the bottom for airflow. Hanging keeps the wreath round and the bag keeps dust off. If closet-door space is tight, our [hall closet organization ideas](/blog/hall-closet-organization-ideas-small/) show how to free up this kind of door and shelf space.

### 8. Coil garlands around a wrapping-paper tube

Wrap garlands loosely around an empty wrapping-paper tube or a pool noodle cut to bin length, securing the ends with a rubber band. Coiling prevents the tangled, kinked mess of garlands stuffed into a bag, and the tube slides into a bin or along a closet shelf. Label it with masking tape so you know which garland it holds.

## Light storage: tangle-free wrapping that saves January you

Untangling lights is entirely preventable: wrap lights around something rigid, under light tension, before they go in the bin.

### 9. Wrap each light string around a piece of cardboard

Cut a rectangle of sturdy cardboard about 12 inches long and 8 inches wide, notch both short ends, and wind one light string around it, tucking the plug into the last notch. One string per card, cards stacked upright in a bin like files. This takes about two minutes per string and saves twenty minutes of untangling next year.

### 10. Keep clips, timers, and extension cords with their lights

Every light string has accessories: clips, a timer, maybe an extension cord. Put them in a zip-top bag, label the bag with the string it belongs to, and tape or rubber-band the bag to its cardboard card. Hunting for clips in a separate "miscellaneous" bin is how decorations end up half-hung.

## Where to hide bins in a small apartment

You do not need a garage or an attic: small apartments have more bin-sized hiding spots than most people realize.

### 11. Slide flat bins under the bed first

The space under the bed is the best holiday-bin home in most apartments: large, dark, and out of daily sight. Measure your clearance before buying bins — most frames give 6 to 12 inches — and choose flat, lidded bins that slide out without lifting the mattress. Our [under-bed storage ideas](/blog/under-bed-storage-ideas-small-bedrooms/) cover measuring clearance and picking rolling versus flat bins.

### 12. Claim the top shelf and the closet crown

The top shelf of any closet is prime holiday-bin territory because you reach it only twice a year. Keep one bin there per closet if you can, with the label facing out. Above that, the gap between a wardrobe top and the ceiling fits a flat bin or a bagged wreath, if you can reach it safely with a step stool. Never store anything you need to climb furniture to reach.

<div class="tip"><strong>Renter-friendly rule:</strong> Every hiding spot in this guide uses furniture you already own and hooks that remove cleanly. No drilling, no built-ins, nothing to explain to a landlord at move-out.</div>

## The keep-list: photographing setups before packing

The hardest part of next December is not finding the decorations — it is remembering how you arranged them. A five-minute photo session before packing solves that.

### 13. Photograph each display before you take it down

Take wide shots of the tree, the mantel, the entryway table, and any vignette you liked, plus one close-up of any arrangement with layered pieces. Save them in a phone album named for the holiday, and tuck a note saying "photos in phone album: Christmas 2026" into the first bin.

### 14. Write a one-page keep-list for next year

On a single sheet of paper, list what you displayed, what you skipped, and what you want to buy or replace — "need: new tree skirt; skip: red garland, too long for mantel; buy: 2 packs gold clips." Slip the list into the "unpack first" bin. This turns the purge into a five-minute review, because the decisions are already made.

## What NOT to store: heat, moisture, and attic rules

Apartment storage has hazards houses handle with a garage or basement. Knowing what damages decorations matters more than buying better bins.

### 15. Keep decorations out of heat and damp

Never store bins on top of a radiator, against a hot-water pipe, or in a bathroom closet where steam collects. Heat warps plastic ornaments, melts candle wax decorations, and dries out artificial wreaths; moisture grows mold on fabric pieces like tree skirts and stockings. If your only storage spot runs warm or damp, add silica gel packets to each bin and check them once mid-year.

### 16. Skip the building's shared attic or basement unless it is dry and pest-proof

Shared storage rooms look tempting, but they are the riskiest spot for holiday decorations: leaks, pests, and other tenants moving your bins. If you must use one, pack in hard plastic bins with snap-tight lids — never cardboard — and label them with your unit number as well as the contents. For anything sentimental, keep it inside your apartment even if one bin lives visibly atop a wardrobe.

## Mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is packing in a rush the day the decorations come down. Tired packing produces unlabeled bins, broken ornaments, and tangled lights. Give yourself one unhurried hour, ideally the day after the holiday, and pack in this guide's order: purge, label, protect, hide.

Another mistake is buying storage products before measuring. Bins that do not fit under the bed or boxes too tall for the shelf end up as clutter themselves. Measure first, buy second.

Finally, do not keep decorations you have not displayed in two full seasons out of guilt. A disliked gift costs the same bin space as a favorite.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I store Christmas decorations in a really small apartment?

Start with the purge, then use one labeled bin per holiday and hide it under the bed or on a closet's top shelf. Flat, lidded bins sized to your clearance fit spaces bulky ornament boxes cannot.

### Can I store ornaments in egg cartons?

Yes, for small and medium ornaments. Wrap each ornament in tissue first, place one per cup, close the lid, and set the carton inside a lidded bin. Egg cartons suit shatterproof ornaments best; wrap glass ones individually with extra tissue.

### How do I keep string lights from tangling in storage?

Wind each string around a notched cardboard card, one string per card, and stack the cards upright in a bin. Keep clips and timer in a labeled bag attached to its card.

### Where is the best place to hide holiday bins in a rental?

Under the bed is usually best, followed by closet top shelves and the space above a wardrobe. All three are renter-friendly, need no drilling, and stay reachable twice a year with a step stool.

### What holiday decorations should I not store?

Throw out broken light strings, cracked ornaments, and anything you have not displayed in two seasons. Avoid hot, damp, or shared building spaces unless decorations are in sealed hard-plastic bins.

## Conclusion

Holiday storage in a small apartment comes down to a simple rhythm: purge what you did not use, label one bin per holiday, protect fragile pieces with materials you already own, and hide bins under the bed or up high.
