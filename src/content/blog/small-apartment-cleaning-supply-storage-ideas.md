Cleaning supplies have a way of taking over a small apartment. One bottle of all purpose spray becomes five, the mop leans by the front door, and the cabinet under the sink turns into a jumble of half empty bottles. These cleaning supply storage ideas for small apartments are built for that reality: no walk in closet, no utility room, no spare shelf. Below are 15 ideas in seven simple systems, from a grab and go caddy to a monthly check that keeps the collection from growing back. Every idea is renter friendly and cheap to start. Pick two or three to start.

![Cleaning caddy with spray bottles and cloths](/images/cleaning-hero.jpg)

## The caddy system: one grab-and-go kit per floor

Supplies spread because they have no home base. A caddy fixes that: one address for every supply, and a kit you carry to the mess.

### 1. Build one caddy per floor, not one per room

Keep one caddy per floor, each holding the same core kit: all purpose spray, bathroom cleaner, glass cleaner, a scrub brush, two cloths, and gloves. A 12 by 8 inch handled caddy holds it all and fits on a closet shelf, under a bathroom sink, or on top of the washer.

### 2. Give the caddy a tall pocket for the long tools

A flat caddy with no tall section means the toilet brush and scrubber end up on the floor. Choose a caddy with one tall compartment, or slide a tall cup inside one end to hold brushes upright. When every tool has a slot, the caddy stays complete.

### 3. Park the caddy where cleaning actually starts

Do not store the caddy in the deepest corner of a closet. Put it where your hand lands when you decide to clean: a low shelf near the bathroom door, or the hall closet floor inside the door. If you must move three things to reach it, you will leave it buried. The same approach works in a dedicated cleaning closet; see [cleaning supply closet ideas](/blog/cleaning-supply-closet-organization-ideas/).

<div class="tip"><strong>Start small:</strong> build your first caddy from what you already own. A sturdy tote bag works for a week of testing; if the kit earns its place, buy the handled caddy.</div>

## Under-sink zones for cleaning supplies (the safe way)

The cabinet under the sink is the default home for cleaning supplies, and the most likely place for a leak. The fix is zones.

![Spray bottles organized under a sink](/images/cleaning-undersink.jpg)

### 4. Put a turntable in the back corner

A 10 inch turntable turns the dead space at the back of the cabinet into the easiest spot to reach. Put your daily sprays on it, labels facing out. One spin brings the right bottle to the front.

### 5. Hang spray bottles from a tension rod

A tension rod, mounted about 6 inches below the top of the cabinet, creates a free hanging rail. Hang spray bottles by their trigger heads so they dangle in a row, leaving the cabinet floor clear for the dustpan or a small bucket. Measure the inside width first; most sinks fit a rod between 16 and 24 inches.

### 6. Line the cabinet floor with a leak-proof tray

A shallow tray, roughly 14 by 10 inches, sits underneath and catches drips before they reach the wood. A slow leak from a cracked bottle can warp a cabinet floor in weeks. Keep a small bin inside the tray for sponges and cloths so they dry. More ideas: [small kitchen cabinet organization ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-cabinet-organization-ideas/).

## Door racks and over-door organizers for sprays

Doors are the most underused storage surface in a small apartment. The inside of a pantry, closet, or bathroom door can hold a full row of sprays.

### 7. Mount a wire rack on the inside of a closet door

A two tier wire rack that hooks over the top of a door holds four to six spray bottles. Measure your door first: most interior doors are about 28 to 30 inches wide, and a 14 inch rack leaves room for the door to close without the bottles hitting the frame. No drilling is needed, so renters can fit one in an afternoon.

### 8. Stick adhesive hooks inside the cabinet door for gloves and brushes

Small tools disappear inside cabinets. Two or three adhesive hooks inside the cabinet door give rubber gloves, a scrub brush, and a dustpan a visible home. Use hooks rated for at least 2 pounds each, stuck to a clean, dry surface. When the door closes, the tools hang flat and out of the way.

### 9. Hang a clear shoe organizer for the small stuff

A clear over the door shoe organizer with 12 to 24 pockets stores everything too small for a shelf: sponges, scrub pads, gloves, refills, and cleaning tablets. Clear pockets let you see what you have at a glance. Trim it with scissors if it is too long for your door. See also: [over-the-door organization ideas](/blog/over-the-door-organization-ideas/).

## Decanting: when smaller bottles actually help

Decanting means pouring a product from a big jug into a smaller bottle. It helps only when it solves a real problem: a gallon jug does not fit under most sinks, but a 16 ounce spray bottle does.

### 10. Decant bulk refills into labeled 16-ounce bottles

If you buy cleaners in bulk, pour a working amount into a labeled 16 ounce spray bottle and store the big jug out of the way, on a high closet shelf. The small bottle lives in the caddy or on the door rack, where you use it, and the jug only comes out for refills. Label the small bottle with the product name and fill date.

### 11. Keep one refill station, not five refill spots

Pick one spot, a single tote or shelf, for all your bulk jugs and refills. When the tote is full, you have enough; when it has space, you know what to buy. This stops you buying a second bottle of glass cleaner because the first one was hiding behind the mop bucket.

<div class="tip"><strong>Never decant into food or drink containers:</strong> a cleaner in a water bottle is a poisoning risk. Use only bottles made for cleaning products, and label every bottle.</div>

## Broom, mop, and vacuum storage in tight spaces

Long handled tools do not fit on a shelf and fall over when leaned in a corner. Get them off the floor and onto a wall.

### 12. Mount grip holders in a 6-inch gap

A spring grip holder takes up about 2 inches of wall and holds one broom or mop handle. Two or three in a row, mounted in the gap between the fridge and the wall, behind a door, or on the side of a tall cabinet, turn dead space into a tool rack. Mount them at chest height so the heads hang a few inches off the floor. Adhesive backed holders work on smooth walls and tile, no drilling needed.

### 13. Give the vacuum one upright home with its attachments in a bag

A stick vacuum or compact upright needs about 12 by 12 inches of floor, roughly the size of a large book. Claim one closet corner for it, with its tools in a small zip bag on a hook beside it. The same closet can hold the mop and broom too; see [small laundry closet organization ideas](/blog/small-laundry-closet-organization-ideas/).

## What NOT to store together (chemical safety basics)

Some cleaning products react dangerously when they mix. Keep bleach away from ammonia based cleaners, including most glass cleaners, and away from vinegar: mixing bleach with either can release toxic gases. Store each chemical in its original labeled container, upright, caps tight, and never pour leftovers together into one bottle. Keep cleaning supplies away from food, pet food, and medicines, never in the same bin. With children or pets at home, store chemicals on a high shelf or behind a childproof latch. The EPA's [Safer Choice program](https://www.epa.gov/saferchoice) lists household products that meet its safety criteria.

## The monthly supply check: restock, toss, simplify

Supplies creep back: a new spray goes on sale, a refill gets bought before the old one is empty. A ten minute check each month keeps the system from filling up.

### 14. Run a 10-minute audit on the first of the month

Once a month, pull everything out of the caddy, the under sink cabinet, and the door rack. Toss empty bottles, products unused for three months, and anything with a cracked cap or a faded label. Wipe the shelves, then put back only what fits the zones you built. If something has no zone, it does not go back.

### 15. Follow a one-in, one-out rule with a restock list

When a new cleaning product comes in, an old one leaves: finished, used up, or tossed. Keep a short restock list on the fridge or inside the cabinet door, and add to it only when a bottle is empty, not when you see a sale. This keeps the bottle count steady at the size your storage can hold: the system works because it is sized to your space.

## Mistakes to avoid with cleaning supply storage

The most common mistake is buying storage before sorting: matching bins will not fix twenty bottles in a space that holds eight. Sort first, count what you use, then buy only the organizers that fit the survivors. The second is buying in bulk before you have a home for it; a gallon jug with nowhere to live becomes floor clutter. The third is hiding supplies so deep you stop using them: if the glass cleaner takes a minute to reach, you will use a damp cloth instead. Storage that is easy to reach gets used; storage that is hard to reach gets ignored.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where should I store cleaning supplies in a small apartment without a closet?

Use three spots: a handled caddy for daily supplies, the cabinet under the sink for sprays and refills, and the inside of a door for a wire rack. Together they hold a full cleaning kit without a dedicated closet.

### Can I store cleaning supplies under the kitchen sink?

Yes, with two precautions. Line the cabinet floor with a leak proof tray, and keep chemicals on a turntable or a tension rod, never with food, dish sponges, or pet supplies. Keep bottles upright with labels facing out.

### How do I keep cleaning supplies away from kids in a small space?

Store chemicals on the highest shelf you can reach, or add a childproof latch to the cabinet door. Keep the everyday caddy up high too, and lift it down only when you clean. A caddy on the floor is within a toddler's reach in seconds.

### Is it safe to decant cleaning products into smaller bottles?

Yes, if you follow three rules: use a bottle made for cleaning products, never a food or drink container; wash the bottle if it held a different product; and label it with the product name and fill date.

Small apartment cleaning supply storage is not about buying more organizers. It is about giving every bottle one home, keeping the daily kit portable, and checking the collection once a month so it never grows back. Start with the caddy and one door rack.
